Orchestrator–Workers: The Pattern Behind Most Production Multi-Agent Systems

Most 'multi-agent systems' that ship to production aren't a swarm of peers negotiating — they're one orchestrator LLM that reads the task, decides what subtasks it needs, spins up worker LLM calls for each, and synthesizes the results. The difference from static parallelization is that the orchestrator can't know the subtask list in advance; it has to derive it from the specific input. This is the shape underneath frameworks like AutoGen's group chat, CrewAI's hierarchical crews, and MetaGPT's role-based pipelines, even though each dresses it differently.

Key learning The orchestrator's job is entirely decomposition and synthesis quality — the workers are usually simple, narrowly-scoped prompts. If a multi-agent system is underperforming, the fix is almost always a better orchestrator prompt (clearer subtask boundaries, explicit synthesis instructions), not smarter workers.
